In light of the new Sacramento zoning laws which you can read about more at [Sacramento Zoning Update](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/community-development/planning/major-projects/2040GPZoningConsistency). I wanted to go over floor area ratio (FAR) and what that will look like in Oak Park. While it is not new for the city to have FAR requirements, they are now the primary way that the city is regulating density.

FAR is the ratio between the area of a lot versus the area of the building on the lot. For a longer overview of FAR please check out my other article [FAR Explained Visually](https://awilmoth895.github.io/Website/articles/writing.html#far-explained)

# Overview

The way the city uses FAR to regulate density is by setting up FAR maximums. Meaning in an area with a max FAR of 1 anything building under a FAR of 1 can be built and so forth. In Sacramento the FAR minimums range from 1 to 15 but in Oak Park the FAR maximums are only 1, 2 and 4. as shown in the map in [Image](#oakParkFAR.jpg)

![OakParkFAR.jpg](../assets/far_explained/OakParkFAR.jpg)

The city is also linking building height to FAR maximums. You can see the heights in [Figure 1](#far-height).

<figure id="far-height" class="figure">

| Max FAR | Max Height |
| --- | --- |
| 1   | 35ft ~ 3 stories |
| 2   | 45ft ~ 4 stories |
| 4   | 85ft ~ 8 stories ([see Figure 2](#far-height-distance)) |

<figcaption>Figure 1</figcaption></figure>

For any lots with max FAR of 4 lots with a max FAR of 1 there will need to be a gradual rise to the max height. This is to avoid 85ft buildings next to a 1 story home. You can see those values in [Figure 2](#far-height-distance).

<figure id="far-height-distance" class="figure">

| Distance From FAR 1.0 lot | Max Height |
| --- | --- |
| 0 - 19 ft | 45ft ~  4 stories |
| 20 - 39ft | 55ft ~ 5 stories |
| 40 - 59ft | 65ft ~ 6 stories |
| 60ft + | 85ft ~ 8 stories |

# FAR 1 Area

![FAR1.jpg](../assets/far_explained/FAR1.jpg)

Most of the FAR 1 areas are in south Oak Park. Which historically has had primarily single family homes with a smaller mix of multifamily and comercial buildings. The previous way of defining density was a lot more complex and varied a lot through the area but at a basic level most of the lots were limited to 1 or 2 units on lots with a max height of 35ft, or about 3 stories. Though a third story was required to be tented (a slanted roof) this looks to have been removed and will be covered more in my zoning arcticle.

Now this has been simplified where the amount of units no longer has a set limit, just a max building square footage. In practice what this means is you can now build multifamily housing on lots that previously where limited to 1 or maybe 2 units. The actual theoretical max building size hasn't increased that much but the amount of units that are allowed has.

For example there are a lot of smaller 1 story homes in south Oak Park on lots that based on the current zoning could allow much larger homes, but they would likely need to be limited to 1 or 2 large units. Now these same lots would allow multiple smaller units. 

I want to also note this will mean an increase in density (Units per square acre) over time, but I also want to note that in areas being gentrified the old zoning laws would likely mean a majority of new things getting built would be large single family home as smaller homes would just make the developer less money. Raising property values around it and pushing people out. Now I am not saying that fixing zoning will solve gentrification, it is significantly more complicated than that. What I am saying is that allowing more units in the same amount of space will slow the rise of property values and rent as the supply of housing is allowed to grow as more people move in.

# FAR 2 Zone

![FAR 2.jpg](../assets/far_explained/FAR%202.jpg)

Areas with a max of FAR 2 would allow double the amount of floor area on the lot compared to FAR 1 areas. So a 1000 sqft lot could have a 2000 sqft building. This area also has a max height of 45 ft. This is heigher than what has been allowed before in much of Oak Park but most of the area north of Broadway and west of Sac High had this height limit already.

An example of an FAR 2 building would be the building in image 13 and 14 that fills it's whole lot and is two stores. The buildng square foot is double the lot squarefoot.

Because of the need to max out the lot to hit an FAR of 2 the builds that do actually reach a FAR of 2 are most often going to be 3 story. Oak Park doesn't have that many 3 story buildings but they will likely look like the 3 story buildings I showed in the FAR 1 section, just on a smaller lot or with less open space.

FAR 4

The locations marked as FAR 4 are almost all commercial zoned locations along Broadway, Stockton Blvrd and the north most part of MLK, there are a few exceptions but they are almost all lots next to commercial location. I wont get into comercial zoning here that is for another article but basically these lots allow business to exist in them along with just about anything else you would find in residential zoning.

These are not getting very much change with the new zoning laws and them having a FAR of 4 has already taken effect as of 2024 when the 2040 plan was approved. But before that most of these had an FAR of 3 dating back to 2015. The change that has caused new development along these corridors has been Aggie square and the removal of parking minimums. I don't want to get into parking here but I will say parking requirements often limit new developments from being built at all. An exchange of real housing for hypothetical parking.

But what does FAR 4 actually look like. This one is a lot harder because it really depends on the open space.
